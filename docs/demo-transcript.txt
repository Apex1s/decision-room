- Price sensitivity 0.0209 per $/t (95% CI 0.01769 to 0.02411), first-stage F = 1354.9 (within-nest share F = 359.7).
- Within-grade nesting sigma = 0.4834 (95% CI 0.4075 to 0.5592; truth 0.45): buyers switch mostly between producers of the same grade, so each grade has its own competitive pressure.
- The plain logit alternative would put price sensitivity at 0.03501 (+68%): ignoring within-grade substitution misprices every grade.
- Plain OLS would say 0.01054: -49.6% off. Sellers see demand shocks when they set prices, so OLS is biased; cost shifters and freight fix it.
- Ground truth (synthetic data only): 0.022. Inside the 95% CI.
- Own-price elasticity (average by producer): Atlas -3.45, Boreal -3.84, Cerro -2.86, Dune -2.69.
- Demand shocks persist quarter to quarter (rho = 0.561); the forecast carries them forward.

What this model cannot tell you:
- Substitution follows the nest structure (grades). Buyers who would switch region rather than grade, or long-term offtake contracts, are not represented.
- The game is static, one quarter at a time. Long-term contracts, retaliation and reputation are not modelled.
- Marginal costs and capacities are taken from the input files. The engine checks them against prices but cannot verify them.
- The conduct test compares three textbook behaviours. Partial collusion or tacit regional splits sit between them and would need a richer test.
